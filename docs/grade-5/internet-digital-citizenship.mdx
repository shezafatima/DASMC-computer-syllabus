# 🌐 Internet & Digital Citizenship

*Grade 5 — Digital Problem Solver*

Topics covered this grade:

- **Effective research**
- **Reliable vs. unreliable information**
- **Privacy; scams**
- **Digital footprint**
- **Responsible communication**

---

### ⚙️ Effective research

1. Use a variety of sources like encyclopedias, news sites, and educational videos.
2. Take notes in your own words instead of copying and pasting directly.
3. Keep a list of the websites you used so you can cite them later.

---

### 💡 Reliable vs. unreliable information

**Concept:** Reliable information comes from experts and is backed by facts. Unreliable information may be biased or incorrect.

**Example:** A government health website is usually more reliable than a random person's social media post.

---

### ⚙️ Privacy; scams

1. Be careful with 'Clickbait' — headlines that sound too good to be true often lead to scams.
2. Use 'Two-Factor Authentication' (2FA) if an app offers it for extra security.
3. Never enter your password on a website that doesn't have the 'padlock' icon in the address bar.

---

### 💡 Digital footprint

**Concept:** Your digital footprint is the permanent trail of your online activities, which can affect your reputation in the future.

**Example:** Think before you post: would you be happy for your teacher or future boss to see this?

---

### 💡 Responsible communication

**Concept:** Responsible communication means being polite, clear, and safe when sending messages or participating in online groups.

**Example:** Avoid using 'ALL CAPS' because it can look like you are shouting at the other person.
