### 💡 Computer hardware and software

**Concept:** Hardware is the physical parts of a computer you can actually touch. Software is the invisible programs that tell the hardware what to do.

**Example:** The heavy monitor and the clicking mouse are hardware. The fun game you play and the browser you use are software.

**Why it matters:** If a game won't load, it's a software problem. If your screen is cracked, it's a hardware problem.

**Did you know?** Without software, your computer is just an expensive, heavy block of plastic and metal that can't do anything!

---

### 💡 Operating system basics

**Concept:** The operating system (OS) is the most important software on your computer. It manages all the other programs, saves your files, and makes the hardware work.

**Example:** Windows, macOS, and ChromeOS are famous operating systems. They give you the desktop and the start menu.

**Why it matters:** Without an operating system, you would have to type complicated codes just to open a single picture.

**Did you know?** An operating system is like the principal of a school—it doesn't do the homework, but it makes sure everything runs smoothly!

### ⚙️ Keyboard and mouse skills

*Become a master at controlling your computer quickly and accurately.*

1. **Practice using the mouse to click small icons without accidentally dragging them.** Keep your hand steady and just tap your index finger quickly.
2. **Use the keyboard to type longer sentences, using two hands.** Try to rest your fingers on the middle row of keys (ASDF and JKL;).
3. **Hold down the 'Shift' key with your pinky while typing a letter to make it a Capital.** This is much faster than turning 'Caps Lock' on and off.
4. **Learn to use the 'Right Click' (the button on the right side of the mouse).** Right-clicking opens a secret menu of extra options for a file or folder.
5. **Save or finish the activity.** Use the File menu or the activity button, then wait for confirmation that the work is complete.

💡 **Tip:** When you type, try to look at the screen instead of looking down at your hands.

---

### 💡 Typing practice

**Concept:** Typing practice is doing exercises to help your fingers memorize where all the letters live on the keyboard so you can write faster.

**Example:** Using a fun typing game like TypingClub or Nitro Type for 10 minutes a day is a great way to practice.

**Why it matters:** In middle school and high school, you will have to type long reports. Fast typing saves you hours of time!

**Did you know?** The best typists in the world don't even look at the keyboard. Their fingers just 'know' where to go. This is called 'touch typing'.

---

### ⚙️ Files and folders

*Keep your computer organized so you can always find your work.*

1. **Right-click on an empty spot on the desktop.** A menu will pop up next to your mouse pointer.
2. **Move your mouse down to 'New', and then carefully click 'Folder'.** A new yellow folder will appear with the name highlighted.
3. **Type a name for your folder, like 'My School Work', and press Enter.** Now your folder has a clear label.
4. **Click and hold a file on your desktop, and drag it over the new folder.** Let go of the mouse button to drop the file inside.
5. **Double-click the folder to open it and check that your file is safe inside.** You can even make folders inside of folders!

💡 **Tip:** If your desktop is completely covered in files, it is time to make some folders!

---

### ⚙️ Simple shortcuts

*Use special keyboard tricks to work faster.*

1. **Select some text or a file that you want to duplicate.** Highlight it in blue.
2. **Hold down the 'Ctrl' key and press the 'C' key once.** This tells the computer to 'Copy' the item invisibly.
3. **Click where you want the copy to go, hold 'Ctrl', and press 'V'.** This will 'Paste' the copied item instantly!
4. **While working on a document, hold 'Ctrl' and press 'S'.** This quickly 'Saves' your work so you don't have to use the mouse.
5. **Save or finish the activity.** Use the File menu or the activity button, then wait for confirmation that the work is complete.

💡 **Tip:** On a Mac computer, use the 'Cmd' key instead of the 'Ctrl' key.
