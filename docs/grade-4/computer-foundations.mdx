### 💡 Hardware and software

**Concept:** Hardware includes the physical parts of a computer system that you can touch and see, while software includes the operating system and applications that give instructions to the hardware.

**Example:** The central processing unit (CPU), monitor, and hard drive are internal hardware components. The operating system and web browser you use to visit websites are examples of software.

**Why it matters:** Understanding the difference helps you know whether a computer problem is physical (like a broken wire) or digital (like a frozen app).

**Did you know?** People sometimes think software is 'inside' the hardware permanently, but you can actually erase and replace software anytime!

---

### 💡 Operating systems

**Concept:** The operating system is the most important software that runs on a computer, acting as the manager for all memory, processes, and interactions between other software and hardware.

**Example:** When you double-click an icon to open a game, the operating system (like Windows, macOS, or ChromeOS) is what actually loads the game from the hard drive and tells the screen to display it.

**Why it matters:** Without an operating system, your computer would just be a useless box of electronics that couldn't understand your commands.

**Did you know?** Did you know your smartphone has an operating system too? iOS and Android are just mobile versions of what your computer uses.

### ⚙️ File and folder management

*Organize your digital work so you can easily find it later.*

1. **Open the 'File Explorer' (Windows) or 'Finder' (Mac) from your taskbar or dock.** This icon usually looks like a yellow folder on Windows or a smiling face on a Mac.
2. **Navigate to your 'Documents' or 'Desktop' folder where you want to create a new space.** Click on these locations in the left-hand sidebar of the window.
3. **Right-click on an empty space, select 'New', and then click 'Folder'.** A new yellow folder icon will appear with its name highlighted in blue, ready for you to type.
4. **Type a clear, logical name like 'Grade 4 Science Projects' and press Enter.** Avoid generic names like 'stuff' or 'new folder' so you always know what is inside.
5. **Drag and drop your files into this new folder to keep them organized.** Click and hold a file, move your mouse over the new folder, and let go of the button.

💡 **Tip:** Use an underscore (_) or dash (-) instead of spaces in file names if you plan to share them online.

### 💡 Typing

**Concept:** Accurate touch typing is an essential skill that allows you to communicate your ideas effectively on a computer without having to look down at your hands.

**Example:** By using the correct fingers on the 'home row' keys (ASDF and JKL;), you can write a whole story while keeping your eyes on the monitor.

**Why it matters:** Typing quickly and accurately saves you a massive amount of time when writing reports or chatting with friends.

**Did you know?** Many people think they can type fast using just two index fingers (the 'hunt and peck' method), but learning all ten fingers is always much faster in the end.

### ⚙️ Keyboard shortcuts

*Learn to navigate your computer faster using just the keyboard.*

1. **To undo a mistake, hold down the 'Ctrl' key (or 'Cmd' on Mac) and press the 'Z' key.** This works in almost every program if you accidentally delete something or make a wrong change.
2. **To select everything in a document, hold 'Ctrl' (or 'Cmd') and press 'A'.** You will see all your text or files highlight in blue at once.
3. **To copy the selected items, hold 'Ctrl' (or 'Cmd') and press 'C'.** The computer invisibly saves a copy to its 'clipboard' memory.
4. **To paste what you copied, click where you want it to go, hold 'Ctrl' (or 'Cmd') and press 'V'.** Your copied text or file will immediately appear in the new location.
5. **To quickly switch between open programs, hold 'Alt' (or 'Cmd' on Mac) and press 'Tab'.** A menu will pop up showing all your open apps; keep pressing Tab to choose one.

💡 **Tip:** Always hold the first key (like Ctrl or Alt) down fully before tapping the letter key.

### ⚙️ Basic troubleshooting

*Solve common computer problems yourself before asking for help.*

1. **If a program freezes, wait for a minute before clicking anything else.** Sometimes the computer is just thinking hard and clicking repeatedly will make it worse.
2. **If it is still frozen, try closing it by clicking the 'X' or using the 'Alt + F4' shortcut.** This forces the program to shut down so you can open it fresh.
3. **If the mouse or keyboard stops working, check the cable connection on the back of the computer.** Follow the wire from the device to the USB port and make sure it is pushed in all the way.
4. **If the screen is entirely black, check if the monitor's power button is glowing.** If there is no light, the monitor might be turned off or unplugged from the wall.
5. **If nothing else works, restart the entire computer by using the Start menu or holding the main power button.** A simple restart fixes more than 80% of everyday computer problems!

💡 **Tip:** Never unplug the computer directly from the wall while it is running unless it is an absolute emergency.
