# 📊 Office Productivity

*Grade 4 — Digital Creator*



*A simple digital work cycle*


Topics covered this grade:

- **Word/Docs: formatting, images and tables**
- **PowerPoint/Slides: structure and visual presentation**
- **Excel/Sheets: simple tables, basic formulas and charts**

---

### ⚙️ Word/Docs: formatting, images and tables

*Make your documents look professional and easy to read.*

1. **Select your main title text and click the 'Styles' menu on the top ribbon.** Choose 'Heading 1' to instantly make it large, bold, and properly spaced.
2. **Click 'Insert' and then 'Pictures' to add an image to your document.** Choose an image saved on your computer or search for an appropriate one online.
3. **Click on your inserted image, click the 'Wrap Text' icon that appears, and choose 'Square'.** This allows you to move the image freely and makes your text wrap neatly around it instead of jumping below it.
4. **Click 'Insert' and 'Table', then drag your mouse to create a 3x3 grid.** A table is perfect for organizing information like a schedule or comparing facts.
5. **Click inside the table and use the 'Table Design' tab to add a colorful style or borders.** Adding shading to the top row helps readers easily see what the table is about.

💡 **Tip:** Always double-check your spelling using the 'Review' tab before printing your document.

---

### ⚙️ PowerPoint/Slides: structure and visual presentation

*Create engaging presentations that hold your audience's attention.*

1. **Open a blank presentation and choose a clean 'Theme' from the Design tab.** A good theme uses contrasting colors, like dark blue text on a white background, to be easily read.
2. **Add a title slide with the topic of your presentation and your name.** This should be simple and clear so people know exactly what they are about to see.
3. **Add a new slide for each main point, keeping the text to short bullet points.** Do not write paragraphs! You should say the paragraphs out loud while the audience reads the bullet points.
4. **Insert one large, high-quality image on each slide that supports your text.** Pictures help people remember what you are saying better than words alone.
5. **Go to the 'Transitions' tab and choose a simple effect like 'Fade' for all slides.** Avoid distracting or wild transitions (like spinning or shattering) which can make people dizzy.

💡 **Tip:** Practice your presentation out loud multiple times before showing it to the class.

---

### ⚙️ Excel/Sheets: simple tables, basic formulas and charts

*Organize data and use the computer to do math for you.*

1. **Click into cell A1 and type a heading like 'Item', then B1 for 'Cost'.** Spreadsheets use letters for columns and numbers for rows to locate information.
2. **Enter data into the rows below your headings, like 'Pencils' and '5'.** Keep your text and numbers in separate columns to make calculations easier.
3. **Click in an empty cell below your numbers and type =SUM(.** This tells the spreadsheet you want it to add up a list of numbers.
4. **Drag your mouse over the numbers you want to add, type ), and press Enter.** The cell will instantly show the total amount without you needing a calculator!
5. **Highlight all your data and headings, click 'Insert', and choose 'Column Chart'.** A colorful graph will appear, making it very easy to see which item costs the most or least.

💡 **Tip:** If you see '#####' in a cell, it just means the column is too narrow to show the number. Drag the column header to make it wider!
